1. Purpose

The Nuremberg Prize publicly names leaders responsible for grave crimes who have not been held to account. It stands for the principle of the Nuremberg trials of 1945–46: Nemo supra legem, no one is above the law.

The prize is a public record, not a punishment. It calls for no harm to anyone. Its only demand is that each honoree answer to a lawful court.

2. Category for 2027: Atrocity

The 2027 prize is awarded in one category only, Atrocity: genocide, ethnic cleansing, crimes against humanity, war crimes, and the use of chemical, biological or nuclear weapons. The legal standards are the Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court and the Chemical and Biological Weapons Conventions.

The five further categories (Aggression, Repression, Ecocide, Plunder, Cultural Destruction) open later, each once an adviser with expertise in that field has joined.

3. Who can receive it

  • A person who, at the time of the acts, had the authority to order or stop them, or a ruling body acting together.
  • The honoree must be alive when the prize is announced.
  • The honoree must not yet have faced a court for those acts. Ongoing or recent acts come first.
  • At most one honoree in 2027. If no candidate meets the standard, no prize is awarded.

4. Who decides

Three independent advisers choose the honoree. The founder runs the Foundation day to day but never votes on, attends or sees the deliberations. A board of three directors runs the Foundation's money and legal matters. No one serves on both.

5. How candidates are found

In 2027 there are no outside nominations. The advisers compile candidates only from existing formal findings: ICC arrest warrants and indictments, reports of UN commissions of inquiry, and court judgments.

6. How the honoree is chosen

  1. Evidence gate. A candidate goes forward only if at least one independent formal finding supports the acts.
  2. Scoring. Each adviser scores gravity, responsibility, impunity and recency from 1 to 5, using the published scoring sheet.
  3. Discussion and vote. The scores open the discussion; they do not decide it.

7. Notice and right of response

The honoree receives written notice by 31 August 2027 and has 30 days to respond. The response is published in full and unedited at the announcement. Candidates who are not chosen are never contacted.

8. The award

A 66 mm blackened bronze medal, described here; the Indictment, a document stating the finding; and a victims' appeal naming one vetted organization, to which the public may donate directly.

9. Announcement and the empty chair

Announced on 1 October 2027, the anniversary of the 1946 Nuremberg judgment. The medal is held in escrow on an empty chair in the dock, and handed over only if the honoree appears before a lawful court.

10. Safeguards

Published evidence; attributed wording that never declares guilt; legal review before every announcement; neutrality across all governments; no incitement of any kind.

11. Funding

No money from governments, political parties, companies or anyone connected to a candidate. Donors above $1,000 a year are named publicly, and never influence selection.

12. Secrecy and records

Candidate lists, scores and deliberations are sealed for 50 years and deposited with a university or national archive.

13. Amendments and growth

Amended by two-thirds of the board with a majority of the advisers. No adviser may be removed for how they voted. As people join, the panel grows into a five-member Committee.
