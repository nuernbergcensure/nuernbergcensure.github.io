Nemo supra legem · No one is above the law

A public record for leaders who have escaped justice.

The Nuremberg Censure names leaders responsible for grave crimes who have not been held to account. Every award rests on formal findings by recognized bodies, the full evidence is published, and every honoree may respond.

Read the charter How it works

First announcement: 1 October 2027

The anniversary of the 1946 Nuremberg judgment. The first award is in the category of Atrocity.

Three commitments

Evidence first

Every award must rest on at least one formal finding, such as an International Criminal Court arrest warrant, a UN commission of inquiry or a court judgment. The full dossier is published.

Fair process

Each honoree is notified before the announcement and has 30 days to respond. Their response is published in full and unedited, alongside the citation.

Independent

No money from governments, political parties or companies. Independent advisers choose the honoree; the founder never votes. The criteria apply to allies and adversaries alike.

Six categories

Each category rests on an existing legal standard. They open one at a time, as advisers with expertise in each field join.

A public record, not a verdict

The prize imposes no penalty and calls for no harm to anyone. Its only demand is that each honoree answer to a lawful court. The medal is held in escrow, on an empty chair in the dock, and handed over only if they do.
