The process

How an honoree is chosen

Five steps, from evidence to announcement. Each one is set out in the charter, and the forms used are published below.

  1. 01 April – May

    The evidence gate

    Advisers compile candidates only from formal findings by recognized bodies: ICC arrest warrants and indictments, UN commissions of inquiry, and court judgments. A candidate without at least one such finding goes no further, however grave the acts.

  2. 02 May – June

    Scoring

    Each adviser scores every remaining candidate from 1 to 5 on four equally weighted factors: gravity, personal responsibility, impunity and recency. Advisers with any tie to a candidate step aside.

  3. 03 By 31 July

    Discussion and vote

    The scores open the discussion; they do not decide it. All advisers vote. Individual scores and votes stay sealed for 50 years; the published dossier says which factors decided the award.

  4. 04 By 31 August

    Notice and right of response

    The honoree receives written notice and the full dossier, and has 30 days to respond. If the response shows a factual error, the award can be corrected or withheld. Candidates who are not chosen are never contacted.

  5. 05 1 October

    Announcement

    The citation, the full evidence dossier and the honoree's unedited response are published together, on the anniversary of the 1946 Nuremberg judgment.

Safeguards

Attributed wording

Every claim is attributed to its source. A citation never declares anyone guilty.

Legal review

A lawyer reviews each citation and dossier before it is announced.

Neutrality

The same standard applies to allies and adversaries of every government.

No incitement

Nothing in the Censure suggests violence. Any threat made in its name is condemned.
