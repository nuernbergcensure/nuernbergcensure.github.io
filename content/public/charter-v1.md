# The Nuremberg Censure — Charter v1 (Launch Edition)

*For the 2027 cycle. Adopted by the board and the advisers on: ____________*

This is the short charter for launching with one founder, three advisers and no budget. The full draft charter is the long-term goal. Where the two differ, this version governs until it is amended (section 13).

## 1. Purpose

The Nuremberg Censure publicly names leaders responsible for grave crimes who have not been held to account. It stands for the principle of the Nuremberg trials of 1945–46: *Nemo supra legem*, no one is above the law.

The prize is a public record, not a punishment. It calls for no harm to anyone. Its only demand is that each honoree answer to a lawful court.

## 2. Category for 2027: Atrocity

The 2027 prize is awarded in one category only, **Atrocity**: genocide, ethnic cleansing, crimes against humanity, war crimes, and the use of chemical, biological or nuclear weapons. The legal standards are the Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court and the Chemical and Biological Weapons Conventions.

The award is titled *The Nuremberg Censure for Atrocity*. The five further categories in the full charter (Aggression, Repression, Ecocide, Plunder, Cultural Destruction) open later, each once an adviser with expertise in that field has joined.

## 3. Who can receive it

- A person who, at the time of the acts, had the authority to order or stop them: a head of state or government, a minister, a commander, or the leader of an armed group or ruling body. A ruling body acting together, such as a junta, may also be named.
- The honoree must be alive when the prize is announced.
- The honoree must not yet have faced a court for those acts. The acts may date from any time, but ongoing or recent acts (within five years) come first.
- At most one honoree in 2027. If no candidate meets the standard, no prize is awarded.

## 4. Who decides

- **The advisers.** Three independent advisers choose the honoree. One of them acts as secretary. Each discloses any conflict of interest and steps aside from any candidate they are tied to, including any candidate from their own country.
- **The founder.** Runs the Foundation day to day: recruiting, the website, paperwork and communications. The founder never votes on, attends or sees the deliberations.
- **The board.** Three directors (the founder and two independent members) run the Foundation's money and legal matters. No one serves on both the board and the advisers' panel.

## 5. How candidates are found

In 2027 there are no outside nominations. The advisers compile candidates only from existing formal findings: ICC arrest warrants and indictments, reports of UN commissions of inquiry, and court judgments. The candidate list is sealed.

## 6. How the honoree is chosen

1. **Evidence gate.** A candidate goes forward only if at least one independent formal finding supports the acts. This is pass or fail.
2. **Scoring.** Each adviser scores each remaining candidate from 1 to 5 on gravity, responsibility, impunity and recency, using the adviser scoring sheet. All four factors weigh equally.
3. **Discussion and vote.** The scores open the discussion; they do not decide it. All three advisers must take part in the vote.

If the vote is tied, the advisers discuss again and vote a second time. If it is still tied, the candidate with the stronger evidence is chosen. If the evidence is equal, no award is made.

**Deadline:** the honoree is chosen by 31 July 2027.

## 7. Notice and right of response

The honoree receives written notice by 31 August 2027 and has 30 days to respond. The response is published in full and unedited, alongside the citation, at the announcement. If it shows a factual error in the dossier, the advisers may correct or withhold the award before announcement. Candidates who are not chosen are never contacted.

## 8. The award

- **The medal.** 66 mm, blackened bronze. Front: Joachim von Ribbentrop in the dock, with "CONVICTED · 1 OCT 1946". Back: the empty dock of Courtroom 600, with *Nemo supra legem*. The honoree's name, year and category are engraved.
- **The Indictment.** A document in the style of a bill of indictment, giving the honoree's name, the category and a one-sentence citation of the acts.
- **The victims' appeal.** The Indictment names one vetted victims' organization connected to the case, and the announcement asks the public to donate to it directly. The Foundation never handles that money. The organization must have no ties to any political party, government or armed group, must have at least two years of operating history and financial statements, and must pass screening against US sanctions lists.

## 9. Announcement and the empty chair

The prize is announced on **1 October 2027**, the anniversary of the 1946 Nuremberg judgment. There is no physical ceremony in 2027. The medal is photographed on an empty chair in a dock for the announcement. It is held in escrow and handed over only if the honoree appears before a lawful court for the acts cited, when it becomes part of that court's record.

## 10. Safeguards

1. **Published evidence.** The full evidence dossier is published with the award.
2. **Wording.** The citation states documented findings and attributes each to its source. It never declares anyone guilty of a crime.
3. **Legal review.** A lawyer, or a law-school media law clinic, reviews the citation and dossier before the announcement.
4. **Neutrality.** The criteria apply equally to allies and adversaries of any government, including the founder's own.
5. **No incitement.** No image, word or event suggests execution or violence. The Foundation condemns any threat made in the prize's name.
6. **Name.** "Nobel" never appears in the prize's name, logo or slogans. The City of Nuremberg's Human Rights Office is contacted before launch.

## 11. Funding

- No money from governments, state-owned companies, political parties, campaigns, candidates, elected officials, lobbying firms, armed groups, or anyone connected to a candidate.
- No company money at launch. Individuals and private, non-government foundations are welcome.
- Donors over $1,000 a year are named publicly. Once annual income passes $20,000, no donor may give more than 25% of it.
- Donors never see deliberations, get no naming rights, and cannot earmark gifts for or against any candidate.
- The founder takes no salary. Donated professional help is disclosed like cash.
- **What the money pays for:** legal costs (review before announcement, defense, insurance); the medal and the Indictment; evidence work and translation; running the Foundation; and advisers' and board members' receipted expenses. Never: payment for evidence or testimony, lobbying or election activity, legal action against honorees, or anything that benefits a nominee.
- **Legal reserve:** 25% of every donation goes into a legal reserve until it reaches a target set by the board.
- Donors may restrict a gift to a purpose, such as the legal reserve, but never to or against a candidate, a category or an organization.
- **No victims' grants in 2027.** Grants from the Foundation's own funds to victims' organizations start only after 501(c)(3) status, legal review, a full legal reserve, and the full charter's victims' grant rules (organizations only, never individuals; US-registered charities; sanctions checks; a written agreement; published results).

## 12. Secrecy and records

The candidate list, scores and deliberations are stored encrypted and sealed for 50 years. Within five years they are deposited, still sealed, with a university or national archive.

## 13. Amendments and growth

- This charter can be amended by a two-thirds vote of the board together with a majority of the advisers. Changes made after a cycle's selection has begun take effect in the next cycle.
- No adviser may be removed for how they voted.
- As more people join, the board may grow to five or seven directors, and the advisers' panel becomes the five-member Nuremberg Committee once five qualified people are available. The provisions of the full charter (outside nominations, further categories, a ceremony, a Foundation-paid sum) are adopted step by step through this amendment process.
